# Session 1c report (2026-09-29): couples games and the Ari & Jo series

The owner asked for 2 high-octane couple-relationship games grounded in the research behind *Fight Right* (Gottman & Gottman, 2024). Then, mid-session, they asked for a series: one couple, many life situations, one game per situation, the player controls both partners, every action moves one partner up or down, the goal is balance, with emotional moments and worst-case scenarios.

## What's done
- **Research:** `docs/research/2026-09-29-couples-conflict.md`, sourced and with the verified facts. It covers:
  - the book's five fights
  - flooding (~100 bpm, breaks of 20+ min)
  - the four horsemen and their antidotes
  - the 96% start-up prediction and the 5:1 ratio
  - 86% vs 33% turning toward
  - 69% perpetual problems
  - common conflict topics
  - the 67% post-baby dip
  - accepting influence and the aftermath of a fight
- **`games/littlemoments`:** a bids-for-connection tap frenzy.
  - Round bubbles are bids; square phones are traps.
  - The emotional bank drains; the couple lean in or drift apart.
  - A live "turned toward" gauge with 33% and 86% markers.
- **`games/floodline`:** a flooding reflex game.
  - The horsemen are symbols you answer with antidotes; you accept repairs.
  - The water level is your heart rate; at 100 bpm you're flooded and hold to breathe.
  - Gentle start-up ×2, and the 5:1 ratio on the end card.
- **`templates/duo-balance`**, the Ari & Jo series engine:
  - Left/right answer cards; each answer moves ONE partner's meter. Buttons show *who* is affected, not which way.
  - Three card kinds: horseman vs antidote, bids, and needs-vs-needs dilemmas (perpetual problems).
  - A seesaw shows the imbalance, and there is one spotlit climax card per situation.
  - Worst case: someone shuts down, or it stays one-sided. Best-case scenes too.
  - Seeded, fair card order and layout; a shared test suite.
- **Series bible:** `docs/series/ari-and-jo.md`, with the characters, rules and a roadmap of 5 more situations.
- **Situations:** `games/arijo-moving` (Moving Day), `games/arijo-money` (Money Talk), `games/arijo-baby` (First Baby · 3 AM), 20 cards each.
- **Reviews** (game-reviewer ×2), with every blocking finding fixed:
  - Little Moments: the first bubble was invisible before the first tap.
  - Ari & Jo:
    - text was cut off
    - two cards taught the wrong lesson ("My way" rewarded as a standoff; refusing a break rewarded)
    - Jo was always the one at fault
    - card order depended on answer speed
    - the docs were garbled.
  - The series was also renamed from "Ren & Jo" to **"Ari & Jo"**, because "Ren & …" echoes a well-known cartoon duo.

## Verified vs mocked
- **Verified:**
  - `npm run check`: 149 unit tests; 11 games under the size budget (≤ 15.5 KB gz each).
  - `npm run e2e`: 36 tests (9 games × 4) with no console errors and no external requests.
  - Series tests:
    - fair card order and layout for fast vs slow players over the whole round
    - text fits (answers ≤ 20 chars, prompts ≤ 56)
    - the first card rewards any tap
    - both partners are hurt, and both slip
    - every tag, card and scene string exists.
- **Bot balance:** the Ari & Jo bot knows every effect and makes about 10% mistakes. It reaches the best case on about 90–95% of days. Real players must *infer* the direction, so expect far lower; playtest needed.
- **Mocked / not verified:** human readability of text cards under time pressure (a known risk to the 3-second rule), audio on a phone, channel SDKs.

## Open questions

- Is 60 s right for a narrative situation, or would 90 s give the climax more room?
